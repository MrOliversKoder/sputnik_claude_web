Sputnik 1

A polished metal sphere the size of a beach ball that started the Space Age.

What was Sputnik?

Sputnik 1 was the first artificial Earth satellite, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957 from the Baikonur Cosmodrome aboard an R-7 rocket. Its name simply means "fellow traveler" in Russian. The program was led by chief designer Sergei Korolev.

Key facts

  • Four external antennas, roughly 2.4 to 2.9 m long, broadcast a radio "beep-beep" signal.
  • It transmitted on 20.005 and 40.002 MHz, so amateur radio operators worldwide could pick it up.
  • Its batteries lasted about three weeks; the satellite re-entered the atmosphere on 4 January 1958.

Timeline

  1. 4 Oct 1957 Sputnik 1 launches and shocks the world.
  2. 3 Nov 1957 Sputnik 2 carries the dog Laika, the first animal in orbit.
  3. Jan 1958 Sputnik 1 burns up on re-entry.
  4. 1958 The US founds NASA, partly in response to the "Sputnik crisis".
  5. 1961 Yuri Gagarin becomes the first human in space.

Why it mattered

Sputnik kicked off the Space Race. It led to major investment in science education and research, the creation of NASA and DARPA, and eventually to satellite communications, GPS and weather forecasting.
